How to Analyze Competitors and Find Market Opportunities

Before opening a business or launching a product, it is important to understand who is already operating in your market. Competitive analysis is not about copying other businesses. It helps you understand the environment, identify patterns, and find opportunities to offer something different.

Starting Toolkit · 2026-11-13

1. Identify your competitors

Research businesses that offer similar products or services. Consider direct competitors, who serve the same audience with similar solutions, and indirect competitors, who meet the same need in a different way.

2. Compare products and services

Look at product range, perceived quality, unique features, payment options, customer service, and buying experience. The way a business serves and engages with customers is also part of its value proposition.

3. Research pricing and positioning

Compare prices and understand how each business positions itself. Some compete on price. Others stand out through quality, convenience, specialization, or customer experience. Your goal does not have to be charging less. Focus on delivering relevant value.

4. Review customer feedback

Online reviews can reveal strengths and recurring problems. Look at what customers praise, which complaints appear frequently, and what needs seem to be overlooked.

5. Find your competitive advantage

Ask what you can offer differently, whether there is an underserved audience, how you could improve the buying experience, and which customer needs competitors do not address well. Better execution or more personal service can make a difference.

Organize your ideas before you start

Competitor analysis is only the beginning. The most important step is using what you learn to make decisions about your product, pricing, audience, and sales strategy.
